\section{E3: larger-data replication and balancing controls}
\label{sec:e3}
\subsection{Transferred training and factorial diagnostic}
E3 freezes its protocol on 3 October 2026 and trains 30 ML-1M models using
the selected E1 settings without retuning: Full, FixedB-r8, and Two-r8;
targets one and two; five seeds; 50 rounds. Local rate $5$, two local epochs,
$C=1$, and $q=0.1$ are unchanged. Full and FixedB retain server rate $1$,
while Two-r8 retains $0.5$. The same noise multipliers give the same
accountant bounds, but the larger $qN$ reduces coordinate noise after
normalization. Dataset size also changes the task, so differences in NDCG
across datasets are not directly interpretable as a population-size effect.

The principal diagnostic crosses two datasets, two privacy labels, five
seeds, two pulse draws, two noise couplings, and two continuation-balancing
choices for Two-r8: 160 probes. Forty ML-1M Full/FixedB reference probes
bring the total to 200. Pulse amplitude is one. Observer sets contain 128
users selected before outcomes are inspected. All users remain eligible
for local updates. These streams and observer sets differ from E2, so
their means need not reproduce E2's numerical values exactly.

The no-balancing condition starts from the same balanced round-50 checkpoint
and disables balancing only during the pulse/continuation experiment. It
is not a model independently trained without balancing from initialization.
Unlike merely changing a joint coupling, this changes the continuation
algorithm itself. That distinction is central to its interpretation.

\subsection{Main diagnostic result}
\input{tables/e3_churn_main}
With SVD balancing, raw-coordinate coupling produces much larger shared-reset
top-10 disagreement than aligned coupling on both datasets and privacy
labels (\cref{tab:e3churn}). On ML-1M, for example, target-one means are
$5.2344$\% versus $0.0625$\%. Without continuation balancing, raw and
aligned churn are identical at saved precision for every seed. Small score
RMS differences remain through float32 arithmetic.

These controls support a dependence on balancing-induced factor-basis changes.
The initial large Two-r8 signal is not evidence for a general intrinsic
failure of persistent local personalization. The result also does not mean
balancing should simply be removed from a deployed optimizer: this stage
does not compare independently trained balanced and unbalanced methods at
equal privacy, utility, and convergence budgets.

The aligned diagnostic is small in ranking terms. ML-1M Full and FixedB at
target one have mean shared-reset churn $0.1328$\% and $0.0859$\%, while
aligned Two retains a score RMS about $3.6$\% of its initial pulse. In the
128-user observer sample, mean validation-NDCG changes under SVD/aligned
Two are approximately $0.000040$ and $0.000226$ on ML-100K, and
$0.000018$ and $0$ on ML-1M at targets one and two. These discrete, small-
sample values do not establish equivalence, improvement, or absence of
effects below their resolution.

\subsection{Checks and research decision}
All 30 training runs and 200 probes complete without failures. Twelve
synthetic cases cover balanced-round identity, reset identities, zero
pulses, and matching random streams. Maximum relative alignment product
error is $3.9127\times10^{-8}$. The saved-evidence audit recomputes seed
summaries from 2,800 observations and validation means from 181,050 saved
user ranks, without scoring additional models.

The broad instability interpretation is rejected. A narrower empirical
conclusion survives: representation-dependent coupling can dominate a
particular paired noise-attribution diagnostic. Its scientific value is
that it changes how the preceding experiment should be interpreted.
Establishing a paper-level contribution would require showing that this
issue materially changes conclusions or design decisions beyond the
present simulator, in relation to existing gauge and alignment methods.
